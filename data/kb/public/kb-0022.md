You can require every member of your workspace to enable two-factor authentication before they can access pages, projects, or tasks. This setting is available on Team and Business plans and applies to all current and future members, ensuring that every account is protected by a second factor at sign-in.

## Before you start

Two-factor authentication (TOTP) is available on every plan, and enabling it issues 10 single-use recovery codes. Members who have not yet set up 2FA will be prompted to do so the next time they sign in after you turn on the requirement. They cannot access the workspace until they complete the setup.

Only workspace Owners and Admins can change the 2FA requirement. If you are on the Free plan, this setting is not available—individual members can still enable 2FA for their own accounts, but you cannot enforce it workspace-wide.

## Steps

1. Sign in to Quillstack at app.quillstack.com and open the workspace where you want to require 2FA.
2. Go to **Settings > Security > Require 2FA**.
3. Toggle the setting to **On**.
4. Click **Save changes**.

The requirement takes effect immediately. Members who are already signed in remain signed in until their session expires, but they will be required to set up 2FA the next time they sign in. Web sessions expire after 30 days of inactivity, and mobile app sessions expire after 90 days.

## How it works

When you enable the 2FA requirement, every member without 2FA on their account sees a setup screen at their next sign-in. They must scan the QR code with an authenticator app (such as Authy, Google Authenticator, or 1Password) and enter a six-digit code to complete the setup. After setup, they receive 10 single-use recovery codes, which they should save in a secure location.

After the requirement is turned on, new members invited to the workspace are prompted to set up 2FA during their first sign-in. They cannot skip this step or access the workspace without completing it.

The table below shows which plans support the workspace-wide 2FA requirement:

| Plan       | Individual 2FA | Require 2FA for all members |
|------------|----------------|-----------------------------|
| Free       | Yes            | No                          |
| Team       | Yes            | Yes                         |
| Business   | Yes            | Yes                         |

Members who lose access to their authenticator app can use one of their recovery codes to sign in, or they can request a password reset link. Password reset links expire after 60 minutes and can be used only once. If a member has used all of their recovery codes and lost their device, a workspace Owner or Admin can temporarily disable the 2FA requirement, allow the member to sign in, and then have them set up 2FA again with a new device.

## Troubleshooting

**A member cannot sign in after the requirement is enabled.** The member must set up 2FA before they can access the workspace. If they are not seeing the setup screen, ask them to sign out completely and sign in again. If they are still blocked, check that their account has not been locked after 5 failed sign-in attempts—locked accounts are automatically unlocked after 15 minutes.

**The setting is grayed out or not visible.** The 2FA requirement is available only on Team and Business plans. If you are on the Free plan, upgrade to Team or Business to access this setting. If you are on Team or Business and the setting is not visible, confirm that you are signed in as an Owner or Admin—Editors and Viewers cannot change security settings.

**A member lost their authenticator device and all recovery codes.** Temporarily turn off the 2FA requirement under Settings > Security > Require 2FA, allow the member to sign in with their password, then have them set up 2FA again with a new device. After they complete the setup, turn the requirement back on. This workflow ensures the member does not lose access to the workspace permanently.

**Can Owners bypass the requirement?** No. When the 2FA requirement is enabled, Owners must also set up 2FA. The only exception is when SSO is enforced with sso.enforce set to true—in that case, Owners keep password sign-in as a recovery path, but they still need 2FA if they use password sign-in.
